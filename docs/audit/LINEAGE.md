# Data lineage of the crop score (read-only audit)

Scope: every input that reaches `evaluate()` (`src/lib/rotation.ts:176`) as built for one field
(page build is assumed identical; the page was not read). Line numbers refer to the files as of this audit.
Labels: **O** observed, **D** derived from observations by a model, **A** assumed (hardcoded judgement), **E** estimated (fitted or imputed).
Scope tags: **L** local (point/cell), **N** national, **G** global/reference constant (Central-European or US reference value).
"Unc." = is uncertainty represented in the number that reaches the score. Nowhere is an uncertainty interval propagated into the score; "Partly" means a distribution over years is kept (mean or driest-30 %) but not input error.

Score skeleton (`rotation.ts:496-501`): `score = 100 * rotation * viability * (0.25*fit + 0.75*(wS*soil + wW*water + wP*profit)/W)`.
Shared intermediate: `yieldShare` (`:402`) = co2 x GAEZsoil x thermal x (1-waterLoss) x (1-0.3 heat) x (1-0.7 cold) x (1-0.35 wet) x (1-0.3 frost).

## 2. Suspicious transformations

1. **National FAOSTAT yield as the local yield anchor** `rotation.ts:417-421`: `harvest = obs.t * clamp(yieldShare/0.8, 0.3, 1.25)`. `obs.t` is a country mean over rainfed+irrigated, all farm sizes, 2019-23. It is rescaled by a physically modelled share that already contains water loss, heat, cold, wet, frost, soil and thermal fit. Result: (a) the national average already encodes the typical drought of the country, then `(1-wf.loss)` removes it again (the authors' own audit calls this the anchor path, `SCIENCE.md` drought section ~l.281-289); (b) `0.8` is one constant for all crops and countries - a country whose real typical share is 0.55 (arid, unmanaged) is rescaled as though 0.8 is typical, which lifts crops that are modelled as lossy and depresses well-modelled ones; (c) a crop with no FAOSTAT row anywhere (`clover`, `cover`; 18 countries for lupin, 14 for buckwheat) is un-anchored and uses `e.yield * yieldShare` (a different scale of yield), so crops are compared on two different yield scales.
2. **TYPICAL_SHARE 0.8** `rotation.ts:93,420,430,431`: used three times (harvest anchor, intensity, cost scaling). A single constant not fitted per crop/region; `SCIENCE.md` says the principled fix (country's own typical share) is "not yet built".
3. **LOCAL_PROOF gate** `rotation.ts:104,496-497`: multiplies the *whole score* by 1 / 0.8 / 0.5. Crops with no FAOSTAT yield anywhere get `LOCAL_PROOF[undefined ?? 'country'] = 1` (full proof) - the absence of data is rewarded relative to a crop recorded only regionally (0.8) or worldwide (0.5). Niche crops get 0.8 (`:496`). `NICHE_SHARE 0.01` was chosen because it "separated the niches ... among the development cells' false picks" (`markets.ts:144-146`) - tuned on the same cells that are scored. The niche denominator is only the area of the 30 modelled crops (`markets.ts:156`), so a country with large unmodelled crops (sugarcane, oil palm, fruit) gets inflated shares. The gate is a prevalence prior inside the "scientific" score, although `practice.ts:1-12` claims prevalence is kept out.
4. **MARGIN_SCALE 800 EUR/ha** `rotation.ts:87,444`: `1-exp(-margin/800)` saturates; reference economics (`crops.ts`) give potato 50 t x 170 = 8 500 EUR revenue, sugarbeet 85 x 40, cotton, sweet potato 25 x 250 vs wheat 7.5 x 210 = 1 575. High-revenue crops sit near 1.0 on the profit axis regardless of detail, small-grain crops land near 0.6. Capped at 1 after the `(1+demand)` factor (`:444`), so the +10 % demand bonus is lost exactly where the margin is largest.
5. **WATER_SCALE = 800/500 = 1.6 EUR/mm** `rotation.ts:89,458-459`: irrigated fields score water as margin per mm of irrigation deficit with floor 50 mm. This rewards crops with low modelled deficit (short seasons, rainfed-in-monsoon) and mixes money into the water axis; the "500 mm irrigation season" is an assumption. Paddy seepage is counted in `deficit` (`water.ts:141`), so rice is judged on water including percolation while others are not.
6. **IRRIGATION_COST 1.5 EUR/mm** `rotation.ts:85,442,450`: same in every country and for gravity vs pumped water (`SCIENCE.md` line ~188 says canal water is priced as pumping). On irrigated cells it can dominate margin of thirsty crops (rice, cotton, sugarbeet, maize) vs low-deficit crops; on rainfed cells `pumped = wf.irrigation` = 0 (budget 0), so water has no price at all.
7. **Binary irrigation from a 0.5 degree cell** `field.ts:95-96`, `irrigation.ts:7`: 49 % irrigated cropland = fully rainfed; 51 % = fully irrigated (unlimited water). The share is a *cell-level cropland share*, not a field-level fact, and the national yield anchor mixes both regimes. Irrigated yield `yieldShare` drops the water-loss term (`:402`) but the anchor is the national average, which contains rainfed shortfall: irrigated fields get the national mean x ~1.0-1.25.
8. **Cost intensity scaling** `rotation.ts:430-431`: costs shrink with the *national* yield level (`intensity` floor 0.3) and with the field harvest. A low-yield country (observed t small) gets seed/machinery cost cut to 30 %, so margin = low harvest x EU price - cheap costs; the model then scores a poor-input system like an economical one. Costs are also scaled by the 15 % local cost index only (`markets.ts:100`, citing USDA ERS US costs, not local data). `e.cost` itself is EU/US.
9. **Price is the EU reference price, not the local price** `rotation.ts:438-439`: at home `rel === 1` by construction, so a country's own FAOSTAT price level never enters; only export-market uplift (<=1.3x, `marketProfit.ts:72,139`) and the world P90 cap do. The code comment (`:434-437`) says this is deliberate (local price levels worsened validation), but then the "market" section and the margin disagree, and local currency/inflation work in `markets.mjs` has no effect on the home-market score. Nominal 2015-2024 prices are never CPI-deflated (`markets.mjs:246-253`), and the P25 over a nominal series in an inflating country biases the *ratio* used for export picks.
10. **Demand adjustment +-10 %** `markets.ts:88-95`, `rotation.ts:444`: `0.1*(imports-exports)/(prod+imports)`. Net exporters (Ukraine wheat, Brazil soybean, Thailand rice) are penalised and net importers rewarded - the opposite of comparative advantage for what a farmer should grow. It acts on the profit axis only, but `profit` carries 2/3 of the mix.
11. **Disease/rotation severities, N_SCALE 400, DEEP_N_FULL 20, HUMIFIED_REF 1.2, 0.3/0.2/0.15/0.35 soil weights** `rotation.ts:106-113,254,471`: all A, uncalibrated, Central-European. With empty history (eval) `rotation = 0.9`, `credit = 0`, `deepN = 0`, so `nComp = 0.5 - needN/400` -> crops with high `nDemand` (wheat 190, maize 180) lose up to 0.14 of soil sub-score vs legumes (nFix) irrespective of whether the country uses fertiliser.
12. **Heat thresholds differ in kind across crops** `extremes.ts:24-32` vs `water.ts:151`: daily >=3 days over T_CRIT for ~25 crops; a monthly `t + range/2 > tMax` rule (e.g. wheat 27) for the rest. Same `0.3*heat` weight in `rotation.ts:402`, so the two rules are not comparable.
13. **Winter vs spring form asymmetry** `water.ts:185,163`, `gaez.ts:141-145`: winter forms have no FAO calendar and `gddNeed` 0, so `thermal = 1` always; spring wheat/barley/rapeseed use *spring oat* heat requirements as a stand-in. Spring forms are therefore penalised for warmth that winter forms never see. *Resolved:* the spring forms use GAEZ's own spring wheat, barley and rape rows (tropical-highland wheat/barley and rabi rape on tropical fields - the extract had simply skipped them), and the autumn-sown form of a mild winter, whose season is built on its own degree-days, is not limited by warmth, like the winter form (held-out check D: south-western Australian wheat #25 from a thermal fit of 0.03).
14. **FAOSTAT 'Wheat', 'Barley', 'Rapeseed' yields (all forms) applied to whichever form the field grows** (`rotation.ts:417` uses `crop.id`, same for spring/winter form while `e` is the form's econ, `:408`).
