# How farmers decide on rotations — literature review

Published studies describe farmers in their own regions and periods. They are **supporting evidence for the design**
of Zixtaro, not a description of any particular user. Our own farmer interview is **separate primary research** and is
kept apart from this review.

| Finding | Source | What Zixtaro does about it |
|---|---|---|
| Practices spread when they are relatively advantageous (profit, risk, workload) and can be tried on part of the farm first; environmental benefit alone rarely decides. | Pannell et al. 2006, *Aust. J. Exp. Agric.* 46:1407 | Margin and drought risk are shown for every crop and plan; the rotation sandbox lets a farmer try a sequence before committing. |
| Across 35 years of US studies, few factors consistently predict adoption; information, capacity and attitudes matter more than demographics. | Prokopy et al. 2019, *J. Soil Water Conserv.* 74:520 | No farmer "types": the farmer sets the priorities, and each recommendation explains itself (ten questions, Shapley parts). |
| Grain legumes declined in Europe despite rotation benefits because of lower relative profitability, missing markets and knowledge gaps. | Zander et al. 2016, *Agron. Sustain. Dev.* 36:26; Preissel et al. 2015, *Field Crops Res.* 175:64 | Pre-crop nitrogen is credited in the margin; what the region already grows (CROPGRIDS) is shown next to the plan; contract crops stay off unless grown locally. |
| Decision-support tools are rarely used unless they are relevant to the farmer's own decision, easy, trusted and fit with advisers; sophistication does not drive uptake. | McCown 2002, *Agric. Syst.* 74:179; Rose et al. 2016, *Agric. Syst.* 149:165 | One decision screen; every number traceable to a source; limits, failure modes and calibration stated openly. |

Limits of this review: it is a short, targeted reading of well-cited syntheses, not a systematic review; most studies are
from Europe, North America and Australia.
